Almost all apps I've used have felt immensely slow nowadays. Developers are pushing more features and more updates, and for what? 

It almost feels like the ease of implementing new features results in a kind of "addiction" to developing new ones. Want to add something to the app? Just one-shot it with Claude Code or Codex!

It's exactly like those incremental games where your objectives keep growing and growing, and suddenly you have a multitude of things to control and grow. It takes you out of the game, you went from clicking a cookie to a whole tree of upgrades and ascension.

I miss the old days when moving a button 2px took weeks or months of A/B testing and user trials. I took part in quite a lot of YouTube and Google trials, and it was awesome to give them feedback that they would listen to and implement (and of course getting paid was nice).

The idea that everything needs to be "optimized" and pushed as fast as possible is just horrible. As an example, Google's hard push onto their AI search feature resulted in them literally removing features. Finding definitions or synonyms for words is much worse because now it's AI generated? They literally had no reason to change it, as the old dictionary embed was perfect.

![definition](/blogs/definition.png)

I hope you can tell that that pisses me off.

Anyways, my main point here is that AI features and use in companies should be slowed. It doesn't matter how much Claude thinks it's a good feature, what really matters is what the users think. The whole point of the application is for the user, so why not prioritize them anymore? Why prioritize pushing features as fast as possible without proper review?
